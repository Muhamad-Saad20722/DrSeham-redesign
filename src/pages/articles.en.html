Asthma

Is Exercise Safe for Children with Asthma?

A common misconception is that children with asthma should avoid physical activity. However, research shows the opposite: regular exercise, particularly swimming, can be very beneficial for children with asthma.

Regular physical activity helps improve overall fitness and strengthen respiratory muscles. Swimming, in particular, takes place in warm, humid air, which is generally gentler on the airways.

The most important thing is to keep asthma well controlled through a treatment plan prescribed by a doctor. Children and their families should also know what to do if symptoms occur during exercise. Consult your doctor to choose the most suitable activity and prepare safely.

Respiratory Health

How to Strengthen Your Respiratory Muscles

Our respiratory muscles work continuously from the first minute of life until the very end. They never sleep or take a vacation!

Keeping these muscles strong can help us maintain better respiratory health and stay active with less breathlessness and fatigue.

Here are some practical tips to strengthen your respiratory muscles:

1. Maintain a balanced diet: Get enough protein, vitamins, minerals, and water to support muscle health.

2. Exercise regularly: Physical activity helps maintain muscle strength and protects against age-related muscle weakness.

3. Avoid smoking: Smoking damages the airways and can contribute to lung hyperinflation, which may interfere with the diaphragm's movement. This can lead to breathlessness, especially during physical activity when the respiratory muscles need to work harder.

4. Avoid unnecessary medication: Some medications, particularly corticosteroids when used improperly or for prolonged periods, can cause muscle weakness, reduced immunity, and hormonal disturbances. Always use these medications under medical supervision.

5. Maintain a healthy weight: Excess weight can put additional pressure on the diaphragm and increase the risk of airway obstruction during sleep. This may contribute to snoring and low blood oxygen levels while sleeping.

Tuberculosis (TB)

Is Lymph Node Tuberculosis Contagious to Family Members?

In most cases, no. Tuberculosis affecting the lymph nodes (extrapulmonary TB) is generally not transmitted from one person to another.

The exception is when a patient also has active pulmonary tuberculosis. This form of TB can spread through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or speaks. That is why patients with extrapulmonary TB are usually evaluated for possible lung involvement.

Completing the full prescribed course of treatment and taking medications on schedule are essential for recovery. If you have been in close contact with someone diagnosed with TB, consult your doctor about the appropriate tests.

Allergy & Asthma

Allergic Rhinitis and Asthma: What Is the Connection?

Allergic rhinitis does not directly cause bronchial asthma. However, both conditions are associated with chronic inflammation affecting the upper airways (the nose) and lower airways (the bronchial tubes).

Inflammation associated with allergic rhinitis may worsen asthma symptoms. Proper treatment of nasal allergies can help improve asthma control and may reduce the need for emergency medical care.

Therefore, both conditions should be properly diagnosed and treated together rather than addressing nasal or respiratory symptoms separately.

Medical Advice

Does a Common Cold Require a Doctor's Visit?

A common cold is usually a mild illness that gets better on its own within about a week, although it may sometimes last a little longer.

It is caused by viruses that affect the upper respiratory tract, including the nose, throat, and sinuses. These viruses include influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and many others.

Common symptoms include a runny nose, sore or irritated throat, sneezing, body aches, coughing, and changes in the voice. Fever may occur but usually lasts no more than two days.

Treatment generally focuses on rest, healthy food, adequate fluids, and supportive care. Depending on symptoms, a doctor may recommend cough relief, fever-reducing medication, or nasal decongestants.

Antibiotics do NOT treat the common cold! The common cold is caused by viruses, not bacteria.

Tuberculosis (TB)

Latent Tuberculosis: Does Everyone Need Treatment?

Latent tuberculosis infection occurs when the immune system responds to Mycobacterium tuberculosis without any evidence of active TB disease.

It may be identified through a tuberculin skin test (TST) or an interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA). People with latent TB infection do not have active disease and cannot transmit TB to others.

Not everyone with latent TB infection requires treatment. However, preventive treatment is particularly important for people with weakened immune systems and those who have had close contact with someone with active pulmonary TB.

The decision to start treatment should be made by a doctor after assessing each patient's individual risk factors. The goal is to prevent latent infection from developing into active tuberculosis.

Prevention

Smoking and Vaping: Their Impact on Young People's Lungs

Yes. Smoking and vaping can affect lung function even at a young age. Both can cause early inflammation in the airways, reduce lung function, contribute to persistent coughing, and increase the risk of respiratory problems, including asthma.

These effects may lead to long-term respiratory problems, even in otherwise healthy young people.

Quitting smoking is possible with appropriate medical treatment and psychological support. Our clinic provides guidance and assistance to help patients quit smoking and avoid relapse.

Medical Guidance

When Should You Visit a Pulmonology Clinic?

You should consider visiting a pulmonology clinic if you experience any of the following symptoms:

  • A fever above 38.5°C lasting more than 48 hours.
  • Symptoms that persist for more than a week.
  • Severe headache, pressure or pain around the eyes, forehead, or cheeks, accompanied by colored nasal or throat discharge.
  • Difficulty breathing or unexplained shortness of breath.
  • A loud, barking cough or a severe cough that does not improve with initial treatment.
  • Ear pain.
  • Coughing up blood.
  • Wheezing or a whistling sound when breathing.
  • Exposure to sick birds before the onset of symptoms.
  • Chest pain or an unexplained skin rash.
  • A weakened immune system or the use of medications that suppress immunity.
